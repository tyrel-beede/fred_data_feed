# The Chain Cracked One Tier Down. The Dial Did Not Move.

*Five defections below the hyperscalers, six forecasts that did not fire, and a capex-bust weight that ended the fortnight exactly where it began.*

Column of November 14, 2026 · built from the `/macro-dials` run of 2026-11-14T06:11 MT (run folder `dials/runs/2026-11-14T0611-dials`, status partial: the Bank of America fund-manager survey, FINRA margin debt and the CFTC positioning report are carried from October and November 3; the term premium was not measured). The oil and Iran readings come from the standoff run written the same morning.

## Lede

On the morning of November 10, SoftBank told its shareholders it had written off about $2.85 billion of advance payments for power equipment it no longer intends to use at American AI data centers — roughly 30 cents of every dollar it had prepaid — and settled another $0.88 billion to walk away from the rest. In the same week Microsoft, the most disciplined of the hyperscalers, was reported to have cancelled a roughly $3 billion data-center deal in England, declined an option to double a Texas compute contract, dropped a letter of intent for 1.35 gigawatts in West Virginia and switched off a 2023 arrangement renting AI capacity from Oracle. Nvidia's chief executive resigned. A Reuters survey of lenders found AI data-center bond deals being quietly shelved. The dashboard that tracks all of this scored five negative events in the capital cycle in twelve days — three inside one cycle is its own definition of a signal.

And then it computed its weights, and the probability it assigns to a capex bust came out at 19 percent at the six-week horizon. It had been 19 percent on November 2.

That is the tension of this column. The bears will say the chain of dependencies that finances the AI buildout — labs borrowing from clouds borrowing from chipmakers borrowing from memory makers — has begun to fail at its weakest joints, exactly as the model said it would, and that the headline weight is a lagging artifact of a method that insists on scoring what did not happen. The bulls will say the method is working: six things the dashboard had assigned real odds to — a hot core inflation print, a tailed ten-year auction, a CoreWeave contract renegotiation, a jump in private-credit non-accruals — did not occur, Treasury-market demand held, oil fell 27 percent without a peace deal, and the average stock went up while the famous ones went down. Both read the same run. The next fork cluster — Nvidia's results on November 17, a 20-year auction on the 18th and the largest IPO in history pricing the same night — will settle more of it than any argument here.

## The week in numbers

The S&P 500 fell 2.0 percent over the month to 7,562 and the Nasdaq 6.8 percent, while the equal-weight version of the index rose 4.3 percent; value beat growth by 10 points. The ten-year Treasury yield ended at 5.10 percent on November 12, down 11 basis points on the month but up 26 on the week, the fastest weekly climb since April 2025; the two-year rose 32 basis points on the week to 4.86 as futures priced a December rate rise at 88.8 percent with an 11.2 percent chance of a half-point move. Investment-grade corporate spreads were 89 basis points (62nd percentile of three years), high-yield 320 (72nd), single-B 339 (82nd, a confirmed move into the stress band), CCC 1,314 (98.5th); the real ten-year yield was 2.86 percent, the 99th percentile of six years. Among the lenses, semiconductors trailed the Nasdaq by 7.1 points on the month, neoclouds trailed hyperscalers by 16.4, homebuilders beat the market by 11.9, and the dispersion of returns among the eight largest stocks reached a standard deviation of 11 points (Nvidia −26, Tesla +6). Foreign official holdings of Treasuries at the Fed fell for a third straight week to $2,524 billion; the three refunding auctions tailed by 0.5, 1.0 and 0.4 basis points with indirect bidders below their averages on all three; US equity funds took in $63.6 billion, a sixth straight week. Brent spot fell 27 percent on the month to $92; the front-month contract settled at $95.84. The event tally: capital −5, debt −1, business 0, one realized hazard toward the benign branch; six forecasts resolved, all as non-events.

## Terms used this week

> **Credit spread (OAS)** — the extra yield a corporate bond pays over Treasuries, in basis points (hundredths of a percent); wider means lenders want more compensation.
> **Real yield** — a Treasury yield after expected inflation, read from inflation-protected bonds; it is the hurdle any capital project must clear.
> **Indirect bidders** — the auction category that mostly captures foreign central banks and funds; a falling share means the overseas bid is thinning.
> **Tail** — an auction clearing at a higher yield than the market was quoting a minute before; the size of the surprise in basis points.
> **Equal-weight versus cap-weight** — the same 500 stocks counted one vote each versus by market value; the gap between them measures how much of the index is a few names.
> **Non-event** — in the dashboard's method, a forecast outcome that did not happen; it still moves the odds, by a computed amount, so that silence counts as evidence.
> **Implied move** — the size of the price swing option prices expect from a scheduled event; the reaction ratio divides what actually happened by it.
> **Branch weights** — the dashboard's three worlds: A, the market grows out of it; B, an AI capex bust; C, a debt crunch; weights sum to 100 at each horizon.

## Bulls

*The strongest honest case: the periphery is doing what the periphery does in every late cycle, the center is funded, and the economy underneath is broadening — the dashboard's own non-events say so.*

### Six dogs did not bark

The dashboard registers forecasts in advance and then scores them. Six came due in this window and none fired. October core inflation rose 0.30 percent, not the 0.4 that would have put the oil shock into underlying prices. The ten-year auction tailed by one basis point, not two; the thirty-year by 0.4. The Treasury's refunding kept coupon sizes unchanged — and dropped the words "at least," which dealers read as the door opening to smaller long-dated issuance next year, the opposite of a supply shock. CoreWeave, the neocloud whose bonds yield 16 percent, reported no renegotiation, deferral or cancellation of a customer contract and raised its capex guide; its stock rose 24.7 percent the next day against an implied move of 16.4 percent, a reaction ratio of +1.51 — the dashboard's own marker for a beat the market was not positioned for. And Blue Owl's flagship private-credit vehicle reported non-accruals of 0.8 percent of fair value, identical to the prior quarter, where the dashboard had put a 60 percent chance on a rise. That last one matters: under the method, a 60-percent forecast that fails is strong evidence against the world it was testing, and it alone pulled the bust odds down by about a third. An expert would check the ledger: six resolved, Brier scores between 0.02 and 0.36, and a rolling mean of 0.118 across eighteen dials forecasts — the method is calibrated, and it said no.

### The 493 are rising

The S&P 500 equal-weight rose 4.3 percent in a month in which the cap-weighted index fell 2.0; value beat growth by ten points; homebuilders beat the market by twelve as mortgage rates fell 29 basis points to 7.11 percent; consumer staples, utilities and discretionary all rose. The dashboard's lens panel calls this the dual resolution of 2000–02: equal-weight and value rose through the cap-weight bust because the users of cheap capacity thrive while the suppliers of it are crushed. For the economy — jobless claims at 195,000, three percent above their yearly low; commercial and industrial loans at a record $2,997 billion; Torsten Slok's observation on November 11 that weekly retail sales stay strong whatever consumers say in surveys — the readings are those of a late expansion, not a downturn. A bust confined to one sector's suppliers is the benign branch for everyone else, and the dashboard's broadening cluster fired at its top tier on November 2 and still holds. The expert's check is breadth measured correctly: the FactSet earnings split between the seven and the 493 was not read this run, and the bull case wants it.

### Transmission has not happened, and the center is open

The bear case lives one tier below the companies that matter. Single-B spreads moved into the stress band — but investment-grade spreads sit at 89 basis points, eleven under the dashboard's 100-basis-point line and 26 under the 115 that would count as a bust element. Hyperscaler bond books were full in the same fortnight: Meta closed a €9 billion six-tranche deal on November 12, Apple sold a seven-part offering, Alphabet priced debut Hong Kong and Singapore-dollar bonds with combined books near $5 billion; the five hyperscalers have sold about $296 billion of bonds this year against a $250 billion full-year forecast. Microsoft's walk-aways are, on this reading, exactly the "returns discipline" a healthy principal exercises when a supplier's grid delays make a site uneconomic — demand is being redirected, not withdrawn, and Microsoft's own capex guide is unchanged. The periphery breaks first in episodes that never transmit: energy credit in 2016 with CCC spreads near 2,000 basis points and no recession, regional banks in 2023 while the index rose 24 percent, small caps from 2021 to 2024. Periphery stress is necessary for a bust and not sufficient; the dashboard's own convergence rule now labels the periphery cluster "chronic" rather than firing it again, which is the method admitting that the same stress for two months without confirmation is a stage note. The critique the bulls are owed: a model that weights a cut four times a hold will always be late to notice that the holds are what happened.

## Bears

*The strongest honest case: the stag hunt is breaking — the first cancellations, forfeitures and walk-aways have arrived one tier below the principals, Nvidia's financing architecture lost its author, and the headline weight is flat only because the method scores silence.*

### Defection has a date now

The dashboard's second game is a stag hunt: every player's payoff depends on the others continuing, so nobody wants to exit first, and the tell that trust is failing is not a default but the rising demand for commitment devices followed by the first renegotiations. This fortnight supplied the renegotiations. SoftBank forfeited $2.85 billion of non-refundable advances on power equipment "no longer in its plans." Microsoft cancelled a Nscale deal, declined a Texas option the Financial Times valued in the tens of billions, dropped a 1.35-gigawatt letter of intent and wound down its Oracle rental, and is debating internally whether to sign any new neocloud deals. IREN cut the remainder of its fiscal-2027 capex to $15 billion from an expected $25–30 billion and said why: "a changing financing environment." Cerebras' first 250 megawatts for OpenAI slipped to mid-2027. A Fermi lease with TensorWave passed its closing date unmet. Nebius, having explored a high-yield bond, took a $3.5 billion secured loan instead and will fund three-quarters of next year's capex with customer prepayments and asset-backed debt — the commitment devices themselves. The dashboard moved the stag-hunt position from 1 to 2 and the arms-race position from 1 to 2 on Microsoft's actions, and raised the stage score to six of seven. Nothing in the bull case disputes a single one of these facts; it reclassifies them as discipline. The expert's check is simple: in 2000–02 the GE turbine cancellations and the Nortel vendor-finance losses were also described as discipline for about two quarters.

### The last node lost its ringmaster

Nvidia is where the chain's revenue finally shows up, which is why the dashboard treats supplier earnings as the lagging node and watches the financing around them instead. That financing was Jensen Huang's. He resigned this week after the Megaspeed affair; the chief financial officer, Colette Kress, is interim chief executive, and The Information reported on November 12 that she "has sometimes pushed back" on deals Huang championed — the example being a proposed $250 billion credit backstop that would have let OpenAI fill eight gigawatts with Nvidia's newest chips. Gary Marcus called Huang the ringmaster of circular financing and wrote that the whole thing might collapse; Michael Burry's note was titled "The Don Goes Down." The stock fell 26 percent in a month; the top-eight dispersion the dashboard uses as a synchronization marker reached a standard deviation of 11 points. The bear reading is that the vendor-financing stage indicator — Nvidia's receivables outgrowing revenue, its stakes in customers, suppliers subscribing $26 billion to Anthropic's IPO — is about to be tested by a management that did not build it, on November 17, against a guide the street has anchored at $110 billion. The dashboard has it at a one-in-four chance of a guide below consensus, with a four-times likelihood ratio toward a bust if it comes, and a new fork at one-in-five that the backstop is withdrawn or shrunk. An expert would check receivables against revenue a third quarter running: that is the Cisco test, and it is the quantity that separates a stage from a state.

### The method is scoring silence, and the long end is not silent

The headline weight stayed at 19 because the dashboard applies a computed penalty whenever a forecast it made does not fire, and this fortnight six did not. The bears' quarrel is with the largest of them: a 60 percent forecast that private-credit non-accruals would rise, registered on a vehicle whose non-accruals were flat at 0.8 percent of fair value, carried a 0.39 multiplier against the bust — more than any single event this fortnight carried for it. That is coherent arithmetic, and it is also how a Bayesian method can watch a stag hunt break and report nothing happened. The debt cycle, meanwhile, moved against both bulls and the method's tiny crunch weight: all three refunding auctions tailed, indirect bidders fell to 55.5 percent at the three-year — the lowest in fifteen months — while Japanese ten-year yields rose seven basis points to 2.86 percent with a Bank of Japan hike priced, foreign official custody fell for a third straight week, UK two-year gilts crossed 5 percent for the first time since 2023, and the ten-year rose 26 basis points in a week to 5.10–5.17 as the futures market priced a December Fed hike into a 3.75–4.00 percent funds rate with core inflation at 0.3 a month. The real ten-year yield at 2.86 percent is 14 basis points from the dashboard's 3.0 line, which carries a four-times ratio toward both the bust and the crunch because the capex hurdle and the sovereign's debt arithmetic worsen together. The bull case's "transmission has not happened" is true of credit spreads and untrue of the cost of capital itself, which rose 71 basis points at the two-year in three months while the periphery was being financed at 16 percent. The expert's check is the 20-year auction on November 18 and the reopenings on December 8 and 10: three tails in a row would be the first crunch element in the ledger.

## Pigs

A pig, in Stanley Druckenmiller's sense, is not a bet on being right; it is a situation where the payoff if you are right dwarfs the cost if you are wrong, and the courage required is to recognize that shape when it appears. Every item below states both sides of that shape and the date on which it will be graded in public. The section's rule is that the payoff is wherever the price has not moved yet — and after a fortnight in which the famous names fell 26 percent and the neoclouds 16, that rule points somewhere specific.

**1. The market prices a Nvidia miss as unthinkable; the dashboard has it at one in four.** In plain terms: the stock that has already fallen 26 percent is about to report, and the question is whether the fall has priced the possibility of a disappointing guide or merely the scandal. The dashboard's fork puts a 25 percent chance on a fourth-quarter guide below the roughly $110 billion the street implies, and a 20 percent chance that the interim chief executive withdraws or shrinks the $250 billion OpenAI credit backstop; the ledger will record the options-implied move the session before. The mechanism: a guide below consensus is a strong (four-times) element of the bust case, and the market has shown how it punishes the name. Payoff shape: if right, a bust element lands and the neocloud and supplier tiers reprice again; if wrong, a beat on a 26-percent-lower base behaves like CoreWeave's +1.51 reaction. Trigger: November 17 after the close; resolves at the November 18 close. Killed by: a guide inside the range with receivables growing slower than revenue. Trailhead: `F-20261117-NVDA-GUIDE` and `F-20261117-NVDA-BACKSTOP` in the run's `forecasts.json`; the chain in `data/options/NVDA_chain.csv` on November 16. *PG-20261114-NVDA-GUIDE: paid if either fork fires; cost if neither and the stock closes higher on November 18.*

**2. The long end is priced for "nothing," and three auctions just said otherwise.** In plain terms: the dashboard gives the debt-crunch branch a weight of 1 percent while the mechanics that would produce one — thin foreign demand, tails, rising global yields — have all moved the same way in a fortnight. The asymmetry: the crunch weight is 1; the dashboard's own 20-year-auction fork is 30 percent for a two-basis-point tail, and it says a tail there plus one at the December 10 reopening would be the first registered crunch element. Mechanism: the cluster's quantities (indirects below average three times, custody down three weeks) filled before its prices, and the week's 26-basis-point rise in the ten-year was the price catching up. Payoff shape: a priced-for-nothing branch pays many times its weight if the long end breaks disorderly; it costs the premium if the 20-year clears clean, which the dashboard thinks is the 70 percent case. Trigger: November 18, 1 p.m. ET; resolves December 10. Killed by: a stop-through on the 20-year with indirects back above 65 percent. Trailhead: `F-20261209-IG100`, `F-20261231-SPCX-FUSE`; `data/fred/BAMLC0A0CM.csv`. *PG-20261114-IG-UNMOVED: paid if IG ≥ 100 on any day by December 9; cost otherwise.*
